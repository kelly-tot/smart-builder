Adult Learning — Section 1.5 · Setting clear and achievable learning objectives

Step 1 · Specific

Who are the learners?

Name the audience precisely. "Students" is vague — "first-year UTT Engineering students" is specific.

Why this matters

The Specific criterion asks you to concisely state what will be done and who will achieve it. Naming the audience also activates the adult "Need to Know" principle — learners see the outcome applies to them.

Step 2 · Specific + Measurable

Pick an action verb (Bloom's Taxonomy)

Choose a verb that names an observable behaviour. Avoid fuzzy verbs like "understand" or "know" — they cannot be measured.

1. Remember
2. Understand
3. Apply
4. Analyse
5. Evaluate
6. Create
Why action verbs?

Verbs like "list" target lower-order recall; verbs like "formulate" target higher-order creation. Concrete verbs make learning explicit and the outcome assessable.

Step 3 · Specific + Relevant

What will they perform — and why is it relevant?

Describe the actual task or content. Tie it to the programme purpose or the learner's professional role to make it relevant.

Step 4 · Measurable + Attainable

How will achievement be measured?

State the assessment AND the standard. Without a standard ("at least 80%", "to industry checklist"), the outcome cannot be judged achieved.

Achievable check

Confirm the learners have the prerequisite skills and resources, and that the performance is observable. Objectives that are unrealistic cause frustration and demotivation.

Step 5 · Time-bound

By when?

Set a realistic deadline — end of session, end of module, end of semester. "By the end of…" works well.
